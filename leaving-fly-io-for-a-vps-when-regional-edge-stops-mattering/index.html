Leaving Fly.io for a VPS when regional edge stops mattering

Fly.io shines when you want machines close to users in many cities. Plenty of internal tools, B2B dashboards and side projects never need that. When almost all traffic is from one country and your database already lives in a single region, paying for sparse machines and volume quirks can stop making sense.

Signals it is time to reconsider

  • You run one primary region and the others sit idle.
  • Postgres is a single primary anyway, so writes are not edge-local.
  • Cold starts and volume mounting edge cases cost more engineering time than the latency you save.
  • Bill surprises come from machines left running in unused regions.

What a VPS move looks like

Pick a provider in the region where your users already are. Run Docker (or a small PaaS on top), put a reverse proxy in front, and keep one staging host. You lose anycast magic; you gain boring SSH, predictable disks and simpler backups. For multi-region later, add a second VPS and replicate deliberately rather than scattering empty machines.

What to migrate carefully

Secrets, scheduled jobs, persistent volumes and health checks all need an explicit home on the new host. Map Fly secrets to env files or a vault, replace fly.toml process groups with Compose services, and test TLS issuance on the new hostname before switching DNS.
