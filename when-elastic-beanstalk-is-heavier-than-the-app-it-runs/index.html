Ops Field Notes

When Elastic Beanstalk is heavier than the app it runs

Elastic Beanstalk wraps EC2, load balancers, ASGs and platform hooks. For a single web app with modest traffic that model is a lot of surface area: configuration files, immutable updates, IAM roles and surprise costs from idle ALBs.

When EB still wins

Regulated environments that already standardize on AWS, teams that need managed rolling deploys across many instances, and apps that must sit next to other AWS services with private networking.

When it is the wrong tool

A SaaS MVP with one or two developers, mostly daytime traffic, and a Postgres instance that could be RDS or a container. You pay complexity tax every time you debug .ebextensions or platform hooks at 11pm.

A lighter path

One or two VPS nodes, Docker Compose or a self-hosted PaaS, an external managed database if you want someone else on call for storage, and a reverse proxy with automatic certificates. Keep the EB ideas that matter: health checks, immutable releases, separate staging. Drop the ones you do not need yet: multi-AZ ASGs and custom AMIs.

Document the rollback: keep the last known-good AMI or container tag until the new host has survived a week of real traffic.
